Engineering finding
Eight trips were generated in the synthetic inverter model. Immediately before each disconnection, L2–N voltage
at the inverter AC terminals (B) rose above the modelled 253 V threshold. The highest 20 ms RMS value at B was
255.3 V. At the same instants, voltage at B was 10.8–12.3 V higher than at the main switchboard (A).
The model attributes most of this difference to resistance in the 185 m PV feeder. A separate brief rise at A takes
B above the assumed protection threshold. Ten-minute voltage values at A remain in the indicative 207–253 V
band. This is not a network compliance assessment.
IMPORTANT — The model uses a fictional inverter rule: disconnect after L2–N voltage exceeds 253 V for about 2.1 s.
No real inverter model, approved grid profile or manufacturer-specific fault code is claimed. Real settings must be
verified before diagnosing a site.

MODEL ASSUMPTIONS
Balanced three-phase, active-power-only model at unity power factor. Per-phase feeder resistance is fixed at 0.095 Ω
and equivalent upstream resistance at 0.070 Ω. The load flow is solved iteratively. Cable reactance, reactive power,
harmonic currents, changing conductor temperature and inverter control dynamics are excluded from the power-flow
calculation.
Voltage and power series come from the synthetic simulation. Pst, Plt and THD(U) are illustrative channels, not IEC
61000-4-15/-4-7 compliant instrument results. The artificial 253 V / ~2.1 s disconnection rule must not be used as a real
protection setting.
FOR ACTUAL FIELD WORK — Verify wiring, PE continuity and sizing, voltage and current connections, CT ratios and
orientation, instrument class/options, calibration, clock synchronisation, inverter model, approved grid profile and
point of supply.

EVENT INTERPRETATION
The inverter model exports 95.6 kW before E-08. Voltage at B exceeds the assumed 253 V rule for 2.08 s, reaching
255.3 V; A reaches 243.5 V at the same synthetic instant. When the inverter disconnects, feeder current and the
resistive A–B voltage rise fall to approximately zero.
These traces were generated analytically. A real Class S analyser and an inverter event log must not be assumed to
have 20 ms relative timing accuracy without a documented synchronisation test.

Voltage-rise mechanism and calculation
E-08 / CALCULATED RESISTIVE FEEDER RISE
Al conductor limit at 20 °C: 0.443 Ω/km × 0.185 km = 0.08196 Ω/phase
Assumed conductor resistance near 60 °C: R = 0.095 Ω/phase
Current ≈ 95.6 kW ÷ (3 × 255.3 V) = 124.83 A
Feeder voltage rise ≈ 124.83 A × 0.095 Ω = 11.86 V
The IEC 60228 value is a maximum conductor DC resistance at 20 °C, not a site reading. The assumed 60 °C temperature
is not measured. Cable reactance and termination resistance are omitted.
A–B VOLTAGE RISE AS PV OUTPUT INCREASES
THREE CONTRIBUTIONS TO THE OBSERVED EVENT
• Feeder A–B: injected PV current raises voltage at B relative to A; this term disappears when feeder current reaches zero.
• Upstream system: exported power can also raise the voltage at A. Its magnitude cannot be assigned to a real operator
network from this model.
• Brief voltage change at A: an additional common rise appears just before the trips; its physical cause is intentionally
unspecified.

09 / 10
GRIDEC / ENGINEERING EXAMPLE / SYNTHETIC DATA
Recommendations and verification
01 / DO NOT CHANGE PROTECTION SETTINGS
Do not raise the modelled 253 V threshold as a quick fix. Obtain the actual inverter make/model, approved grid profile,
overvoltage stages, time delays and fault-log meaning. Confirm permissible changes with the relevant network operator.
02 / CHECK THE PV FEEDER DESIGN
Have the designer check conductor cross-section, PE routing and sizing, current-carrying capacity, protection
coordination, termination condition and voltage-rise limit. Compare a larger cable, parallel conductors or a shorter route,
including installation cost and losses.
03 / CHECK INVERTER CONTROL FUNCTIONS
Review P(U) active-power reduction and Q(U) reactive-power control only where supported and allowed by the
connection agreement. P(U) reduces yield. Q(U) changes current and may not eliminate disconnections.
04 / INVESTIGATE VOLTAGE AT A
Log L–N voltage, active and reactive power, feeder current and nearby large loads at A. If warranted, discuss upstream
variations with the distribution operator; this model does not assign responsibility.
05 / VERIFY THE RESULT AFTER ANY CHANGE
Repeat simultaneous A/B recordings at similar high PV output and low workshop demand. Capture weather, inverter
settings, connection details and instrument timing/uncertainty.
PROPOSED VERIFICATION CRITERIA
• No overvoltage-related trip during a pre-agreed verification window with comparable irradiance and plant output.
• Maximum terminal voltage and A–B rise remain within design limits defined using the approved inverter protection profile,
measured uncertainty and operating conditions.
• Before/after datasets, inverter settings, calculations and responsible engineer’s review are archived.
